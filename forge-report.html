The idea that entered vs. the idea that left

It entered as a well-formed solo-dev brief: 3 weekends of evenings, learn-Godot project, Kenney art at M6. It left as a factory-built game with an engineered human-in-the-loop — the same game, but every fuzzy term resolved and the two fighting success criteria replaced by a ruled dual purpose.

Locked decisions

  • Factory is the dev. Never-opened-Godot operator; pi-dark-factory builds from a PRD-sliced backlog.
  • Purpose = B+A: prove the pipeline on a game-shaped payload AND ship a fun game.
  • Ruling: cut product scope before cutting the loop. “Skip the playtest” never happens; “cut the railgun” always can.
  • Fun-sensor loop: staging web build auto-deployed on every merge; slice-gating (next cluster's issues only after the previous build's 3-field verdict); 7 weighted checkpoints, ~2 hours of operator time total.
  • Arena = infinite plane (kills wall-collision code and corner-camping in one move).
  • Art = terminal aesthetic as a theme resource (font + palette + glyphs); Kenney survives as audio packs only.
  • Upgrade pool: no slot limits, owned-first weighting, +30 HP filler. Elite: scaled Tank, HP bar, XP bomb, no new AI.
  • Autoplay verdict gate (headless bot, outcome assertions) is a Phase 0–1 issue, not a polish item.
  • Tuning target: victory ≈ run 3–5, minute 1 easy, minute 14 desperate, curves as data.

Rejected options and why

  • Bounded arena — pays code + art for a wall that creates the corner-camping degenerate strategy; geography is not content here.
  • Generic Kenney sprite art — no dev/bug sprites exist in the packs; the title-tagline-art triangle would never close, and detail hurts readability at 300 entities.
  • In-house pixel art — costs human art hours the operator does not have; factory-generated pixel art is a quality lottery.
  • “Learn Godot” as the stated purpose — falsified in session: nobody in the loop hand-writes the GDScript. Replaced by B+A.
  • Slicing the whole backlog up front — the factory would compound unfun past an unplayed checkpoint; verdict-gated slicing adopted instead.

Weak points that survived scrutiny (honest residue)

  • The autoplay gate cannot feel fun. It asserts outcomes, not enjoyment. The entire fun burden stays on 7 human checkpoints — if the operator procrastinates those, nothing in the pipeline will catch it.
  • Web-export performance is unproven until Phase 4 profiling; the ~300-entity cap may need to drop on HTML5.
  • The factory environment has no Godot — Phase 0's toolchain issue and the env.mode decision are load-bearing; a miss here burns laps.
  • “Fun at minute 10” is still a hypothesis. The brief's own success criterion is now measurable but not yet true.

Credits — voices that pressure-tested the idea

  • The Foreman — factory-lap veteran; named verify-gate theater and forced the question “who plays this thing, and when?”
  • The Basher — early-access playtest runner; identified the operator's attention as the scarce resource and the zero-cost staging build as the fix.
  • A survivors-like designer — ruled the bounded arena out on degenerate-strategy grounds.
  • The art director — turned the Kenney contradiction into the terminal aesthetic.
